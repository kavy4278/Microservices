# AnnBachao: A Mentor's Plan to Fill the Gaps

How I would proceed if this were my company, thinking as an investor, mentor and owner.

**Memory trick: Prove, Pay, Profit.** Prove the rescue works, find who will pay, and only then chase profit.

My view is that the current idea is **too big for a first business**. Eight stakeholders, stars, paid orders and rider jobs would need a large team and a lot of money to run. I would shrink it to one clear wedge first.

## Step 1: Cut and defer

| Keep now | Defer to later | Drop for now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rescue loop with OTP proof | Paid "Food Drive Orders" | Stars and streaks (they cost money and invite fake listings) |
| Restaurants/hotels, NGOs and riders only | PGs, hostels, shops, Gruh Udyog | "Employment" claims for riders |
| Impact data from verified deliveries | Subscriptions and featured listings |  |

## Step 2: Decide who pays (one rule)

- **Donors:** free.
- **NGOs:** free.
- **Riders:** get a small payout per delivery.
- **Sponsors pay for all of it.** These are companies with CSR budgets, and individuals.

In India, companies can spend CSR money on hunger-related work. Check the exact rules before you pitch.

I would also try **"sponsor a meal"** for individuals. Giving food on birthdays, festivals and in memory of family members is already common in Gujarati and wider Indian culture. A sponsor pays, and your OTP proof shows exactly where the meals went. Test whether people will actually pay, because this is an assumption.

## Step 3: Phased plan

1. **Months 0–3: pilot, no revenue.** Get 10 donors, 3–5 NGOs and a few riders in one area you can cover. Track the numbers in Step 4.
2. **Months 3–6: first money.** Get one or two small CSR sponsors and a handful of individual sponsors. Ask them to pay per verified meal delivered.
3. **Months 6–12: add a delivery fee.** Charge the donor or sponsor a small fee per delivery.
4. **Year 2 onward:** add impact reports, subscriptions for chains, featured listings, and only then paid food orders.

## Step 4: Numbers investors will ask for

- Rescues per week
- Percentage delivered versus expired
- Time from posting to delivery
- Donors who come back
- **Cost per meal delivered**

## Worked example (made-up numbers, validate in your pilot)

1. A sponsor pays **₹5 per verified meal**.
2. The pilot delivers 4,000 meals in a month, so revenue is **₹20,000**.
3. One rider trip carries about 20 meals and pays ₹40, so 200 trips cost **₹8,000**.
4. That leaves **₹12,000** before hosting and support costs.

This is small, and that is fine. Early on, investors care more that the loop repeats and the cost per meal is low than about profit.

## Step 5: Honest risks

- **Sponsors move slowly.** CSR deals can take months, so start talking to them early.
- **Donors may not care about a fee.** That is why they stay free.
- **Volume decides everything.** Without enough rescues, no revenue model works.

## For your viva and placements

A small plan you can defend with numbers beats a large plan with gaps. Say clearly what you cut and why. Interviewers like to hear that choice.
